# Opening Treatment

### Opening title — this user's default

Default to the live-presenter-plus-title opening approved on 2026-10-29 in the Gemini 4 Carbon video. See [the approved composed frame](../assets/presenter-title-opening-reference.png). From the first frame, show the actual speaker's full-frame moving A-roll and a horizontally centered, prominent headline. Keep the original background, framing, proportions, natural skin color, speech timing, and lip sync. The opening is not a still portrait, generated presenter, circular PiP, or graphics-only title card.

Use large, bold type with strong contrast, generally one or two short headline lines, visibly larger than ordinary captions. Place the title in the nearest clear central area below the chin when the face occupies the middle; maintain a separate caption lane. In black-gold, use cream/gold text on a restrained dark support surface. A narrow title strip at the top does not satisfy this preference.

The approved 1080×1920 example placed the title at approximately x=70, y=1250, width=940, with an 81 px main headline and captions around y=1630. These are a starting reference, not fixed coordinates: adapt to the actual face, gestures, canvas and text. Keep the title visible immediately, allow a readable opening hold (about three seconds in that example), then clear it while the speaker continues. Enter circular-PiP explanations only at a later semantic beat. Inspect the first frame, settled title, title exit, and phone-size result for face/title/caption clearance. This changes the video opening only; the separate cover workflow remains unchanged.

### Illustrated relationship titles — explicit alternative

Use this direction only when the user explicitly requests an illustrated or conceptual opening. The earlier Google Cloud/OpenAI handshake example remains a reference for that alternative, not the default. See [the saved reference](../assets/title-relationship-reference.png).

- Start with the subjects, their relationship, and the question or change the opening must convey. Pair a short headline with one immediately readable visual: cooperation, rivalry, an exchange, a bottleneck, or a dependency, according to the actual content.
- For named companies or products, use authentic, recognizable logos or product imagery. Preserve logo proportions and brand colors; follow the applicable media/image skill when sourcing or generating artwork. Let the illustration explain the relationship rather than repeat the full spoken sentence.
- Favor a warm paper ground, clean dark outlines, generous negative space, and a small warm accent, coordinated with the house Ink/Paper/Signal palette. This title treatment can use a light illustrated surface while the rest of the video's visual system remains coherent.
- Treat the reference's clouds, handshake, and skyline as one topic-specific solution. Choose a new metaphor for a different story; decorative stickers alone do not meet the preference. A conceptual illustration must not imply an additional factual relationship unsupported by the source.
- Design the illustration and headline as one reading unit, clearly larger than ordinary captions. Keep the face and caption lane protected. When the footage lacks enough clear space, use a brief full-frame illustrated title beat with the original speech continuing instead of shrinking or crowding the artwork.
- Keep motion purposeful: establish the subjects, reveal the connection, then settle the title. Use plain typography when a visual metaphor would obscure the idea or when the user requests it.
