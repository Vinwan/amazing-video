# Clear Signal Editorial / 清晰证据流

Use this as the house style for talking-head packaging. It should feel authored, editorial, and evidence-led: the speaker makes the argument; graphics reveal its structure.

## Character

- Clear before flashy; decisive rather than frantic.
- Warm editorial materiality instead of glossy tech UI.
- High contrast with small, controlled bursts of color.
- One visual assertion per beat and visible breathing room.
- Motion follows speech logic: introduce, establish, prove, conclude.

Do not treat another creator's edit as the target. External samples may inform pacing or density, but must not replace this system's recognizable grammar.

## Core visual language

### Palette

- **Ink `#11100D`:** main text, rules, dark cards, and deep backgrounds.
- **Paper `#FFF9EC`:** primary light surface and text on ink.
- **Signal `#FFD84A`:** central takeaway, decisive quantity, or active step. Use Ink text on a yellow marker rather than yellow text on Paper.
- **Purple:** `#7C3AED` on Paper / `#A78BFA` on Ink; concepts, AI capability, or methodology.
- **Orange:** `#C2410C` on Paper / `#FB923C` on Ink; action, cost, attention, or a transition that needs emphasis.
- **Red:** `#DC2626` on Paper / `#F87171` on Ink; risk, contradiction, loss, or error. Do not imply bad news through red on a neutral fact.
- **Blue:** `#2563EB` on Paper / `#60A5FA` on Ink; evidence, measured data, platforms, or connections.
- **Green:** `#15803D` on Paper / `#4ADE80` on Ink; benefit, growth, completion, or an actionable solution.

This user's default now includes multicolor captions and cards, rather than a yellow-only treatment. Select colors by meaning and topic; normally use two or three accent families across a video, with one dominant accent per beat. More colors are available when the story needs distinct categories; do not force every palette color into every video or recolor each word randomly. Preserve a category's color across captions, cards, and diagrams. Keep brand assets in their authentic colors.

Ink and Paper remain the main reading surfaces. Add variety through colored headers, rules, index chips, data highlights, or tinted card panels: Purple `#F3E8FF`, Orange `#FFF0DF`, Red `#FEE2E2`, Blue `#DBEAFE`, Green `#DCFCE7`, with Ink body text. A solid accent card is also possible when its text contrast passes inspection. Do not rely on hue alone to communicate a category; retain labels, arrows, signs, or icons. Check readable text at at least 4.5:1 contrast and large display text at at least 3:1 on the actual footage or support panel. Accent area should stay subordinate to the message.

### Typography

- Use a sturdy Chinese grotesk/sans for spoken ideas and a compatible tabular-numeral face for data.
- Prefer compact, declarative copy. Use weight, scale, and spacing before outlines, glows, or gradients.
- Keep captions at medium-bold; reserve the heaviest weight for hooks, proof numbers, and conclusions.
- Never split numbers from units, proper nouns, or fixed domain phrases.

### Geometry

- Use squared editorial cards with modest 16–28 px corner radii, not floating glass pills.
- Use a 3–5 px evidence rule, bracket, or corner mark as a recurring signal device.
- Use small rectangular index chips (`01`, `02`, `03`) for sequences and chapters.
- Keep borders intentional and shadows shallow. Avoid glassmorphism, neon glow, and oversized soft blobs in the video packaging. The topic-led technology cover treatment below may use restrained dimensional brand cards and edge lighting.
- Align elements to a visible grid. Favor asymmetric balance around the speaker over centered dashboard layouts.

## Composition grammar

- **Hook:** show the actual speaker full-frame with one short, large centered title from the first frame; let a signal block, rule, or bracket emphasize the decisive phrase while the live performance continues.
- **Claim:** editorial headline plus one short supporting label; do not wrap the speech into a paragraph.
- **Evidence:** label -> dominant number/fact -> short interpretation. Make provenance or context visible when available.
- **Comparison:** hold shared attributes still and animate only the meaningful difference.
- **Process:** index chips and one directional relationship; avoid ornamental flowcharts.
- **Conclusion:** reuse the opening signal motif and remove secondary decoration.

Keep eyes, nose, mouth, hands, products, and captions protected. Adapt geometry to verified low-information regions rather than forcing a fixed corner. Classic keeps the moving full-frame presenter and original background visible: all information cards, screenshots, photographs, and diagrams float over the footage. Never replace it with a full-screen card, dark canvas, enlarged screenshot, or PiP layout. If space is insufficient, simplify the information, show a relevant screenshot detail, sequence smaller cards, or omit the graphic rather than covering the shot. Full-screen visual scenes belong to premium-v2 unless explicitly requested otherwise.

2. **Produce:** use that plan and the inspected reference rather than an improvised video-title layout. Compare the resulting face, glasses, hair, clothing and expression to the source; a preservation prompt does not prove fidelity. 3. **Accept:** inspect the actual final image beside the reference and source frame at full size and phone-grid thumbnail size. Record a result and brief visual evidence for every applicable Cover acceptance item in the project's validation report; explain any not-applicable item. Fix failures before delivery. Generation success or a more attractive revision cannot substitute for this check. These are agent checks, not additional user approval steps.

### Person, headline, and spacing

- Choose a sharp, well-lit moment with open eyes, a natural expression, and a useful gesture when available. Extract from this video's cleaned A-roll without baked-in captions or cards; save the clean frame and timestamp.
- Keep the person's face, glasses, complete hair silhouette, clothing, and expression faithful to that frame. A cutout can be scaled down and repositioned; the person does not need to fill the cover or extend across its entire width. Do not invent a different performance or replace the user with another person.
- Start with a dedicated upper headline zone and a smaller half-body portrait in the middle/lower region, adjusting left/right placement around the topic imagery. A head width around one third of the cover is a useful starting point, not a fixed requirement. Keep the person recognizable at thumbnail size.
- Protect the entire head, including the top and outer edges of the hair, ears, glasses, and face. Leave a visible gap between the headline/marker block and the hair. Do not hide hair under text, a fading title background, a card, or a glow. If the headline collides, reduce/reposition the portrait or reflow the title; do not solve the collision by cropping the head or fading its upper edge away.
- Put the central question or payoff into a concise headline, normally one to three lines, with heavy high-contrast type and one decisive color emphasis. A small eyebrow and short footer are optional. Keep brand cards, headline, and footer in distinct reading zones rather than stacking them over the face.
- Match the title to the actual claim and inspect it at phone-grid thumbnail size. Borrow hierarchy and contrast from references, not another creator's identity, watermark, or exact composition.

- For AI/software/technology topics, default to a restrained technology background: deep navy/blue-violet, a fine grid, a few data/circuit connections, subtle document/code motifs, and controlled depth or edge light. - Extract relevant company/product names from the current video's subject and the user's explicit visual brief. Brand proximity or a decorative ecosystem backdrop must not be turned into a claim of donation, endorsement, partnership, ownership, or compatibility. ## Narrative alternation

Build rhythm by alternating three modes instead of stacking overlays continuously:

### A-roll mode

Keep the speaker full-frame for the hook, strong opinions, rhetorical questions, emotional delivery, connective sentences, and pauses after dense information. The opening main title follows the centered-title guidance above. After that title resolves, use ordinary captions and, at most, a small signal mark. Let unadorned footage provide visual relief.

### Explainer mode

In classic, explain relationships using compact floating cards over the continuing full-frame A-roll, not a separate dark canvas. Suitable subjects include people/company relationships, causes and consequences, loops, timelines, revenue structures, competing options, and historical evidence.

- Keep the live speaker full-frame with the original background; do not reduce the speaker to circular PiP.
- Use one central model: relationship map, cycle, evidence image, two-way comparison, or compact bar chart.
- Give archival screenshots and photographs a crisp Paper border and a short context label.
- Use thin Paper connectors and the selected semantic accent for the active relationship, selected node, or decisive bar; preserve category colors when a comparison needs two.
- Use Ink/Paper support inside the bounded card only. Do not replace, blur, or darken the entire source background to simulate a full-screen explanation.
- Preserve the ordinary caption lane below the explanation rather than turning every spoken line into diagram text.

If a model cannot fit without crowding the presenter, split it across sequential cards or simplify it. Premium-v2's full-screen explanations and circular PiP follow their separate reference; they are not classic treatments.

### Emphasis mode

Remain on A-roll and show one oversized phrase, ideally 2–6 Chinese characters, when the speaker lands a memorable judgment. Keep the normal caption separate and smaller. Use this sparingly—normally once per major section, not once per sentence.

### Switching rule

Introduce a floating explanation at the first sentence that establishes the model and clear it when the relationship resolves. Let the unobstructed A-roll carry the speaker's interpretation or next section setup. Avoid adding a card for a single noun or keeping cards on screen continuously.

## Caption grammar

- Treat captions as the speech layer, not as information cards.
- Use Paper text with Ink support or Ink text on a Paper panel according to the footage. Color selected spans with the semantic palette above; ordinary words stay neutral so emphasis remains visible.
- Highlight the key data in spoken copy: quantities, percentages, prices, dates, durations, and before/after values when they carry the argument. Keep the value and unit together, and retain nearby qualifiers such as “近”, “超过”, currency, time period, and comparison direction. Do not highlight every incidental number.
- Normally emphasize one phrase per page. For a before/after comparison, two coordinated value spans are appropriate. Choose colored text, a marker block, underline, or a compact number badge; do not stack all treatments on one phrase.
- Example: in “Agent Skills 已经有 100 万个了”, highlight “100 万个” in Blue or on a yellow marker; in “近 2.8 亿次安装”, keep “近” and “次安装” attached to the value. Use the same data color in the corresponding evidence card. These are formatting examples, not facts to reuse in another video's copy.
- Save highlights as structured caption metadata, for example `emphasis: [{"text":"100 万个","colorRole":"data","treatment":"marker"}]`. Resolve roles to the project's selected palette. Match the exact phrase, specify its occurrence when ambiguous, and do not alter transcript text or speech timing to create emphasis. Render styled spans in the final composition; plain SRT retains the readable text while JSON keeps styling metadata.
- Review a key-data caption and a colored card in the final rendered output at phone size. If a highlight washes out against footage, add Ink/Paper support or use the corresponding contrast-safe palette variant.
- Prefer a quick baseline rise and opacity settle. Do not animate every character independently by default.
- Keep captions to two lines and one primary reading target.

## Motion grammar

Use three phases:

1. **Introduce (0.18–0.30 s):** rule, bracket, chip, or surface establishes the reading area.
2. **Settle (0.25–0.45 s):** text or number arrives with a short rise/slide and decisive ease-out.
3. **Resolve (0.18–0.35 s):** highlight lands, comparison changes, or the proof marker completes.

Prefer hard editorial cuts, short directional wipes, masked reveals, and scale settles. Use punch-ins only at major claims or proof moments and return to stable framing. Avoid perpetual drift, elastic bounce, random rotations, particle decoration, and identical entrances on every scene.

Within Explainer mode, reveal the floating model in causal order rather than all at once: establish nodes, draw the active connector, then land the interpretation. Keep the full-frame A-roll continuous underneath; animate the card's entry and exit, not a transition into a replacement canvas. Avoid page-flip, cube, or spectacle transitions.

## Density and rhythm

- Maintain one primary reading target and at most one supporting graphic.
- Allow graphics to remain still long enough to read; entrance time must be shorter than reading time.
- Do not add a card merely to avoid an unadorned talking-head frame.
- Create visual relief after dense evidence: return to the speaker, a short keyword, or an intentionally quiet frame.
- For long-form short-video essays, aim for a recurring human reset after each completed explanation cluster rather than continuous full-screen graphics. Do not enforce a fixed number of seconds when the semantics call for a longer or shorter beat.

## Adaptation boundaries

Adapt the Paper temperature, typeface choice, pace, card width, and amount of motion to the topic and footage. Preserve the core palette roles, editorial geometry, evidence markers, index chips, caption semantics, and three-phase motion so different videos still feel like the same series.
