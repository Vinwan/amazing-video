# Matching Cover

## Matching video cover — included in every completed package

The user wants one cover for every packaged video: a faithful real frame of the user, a large topic-specific title, and relevant visual context. See [the revised technology-cover reference](../assets/cover-reference-tech-brands.png) for the preferred balance of a smaller person, clear headline space, and recognizable brand elements. The [creator-cover reference](../assets/cover-reference-creator-grid.png) is only a general hierarchy reference. The cover remains grounded in the real video frame, even when its background is redesigned.

### Plan → production → acceptance

1. **Plan:** visually inspect the relevant saved cover reference and the selected clean A-roll frame. Complete DESIGN.md's Matching Cover section before making the cover: reference path, source frame/time, exact headline, portrait scale and full-head clearance, topic background, relevant assets with provenance, and supported relationships. Cover composition is shared across video variants; an explicit cover instruction overrides this default.
2. **Produce:** use that plan and the inspected reference rather than an improvised video-title layout. When using image generation, provide the source frame for identity and the approved cover reference for composition/style, with their roles separated. A rejected cover is only a correction target, not an approved layout reference. Compare the resulting face, glasses, hair, clothing and expression to the source; a preservation prompt does not prove fidelity. Use authentic product/brand assets or accurate wordmarks; label conceptual product imagery as illustration when it could be mistaken for evidence.
3. **Accept:** inspect the actual final image beside the reference and source frame at full size and phone-grid thumbnail size. Record a result and brief visual evidence for every applicable Cover acceptance item in the project's validation report; explain any not-applicable item. Fix failures before delivery. Generation success or a more attractive revision cannot substitute for this check. These are agent checks, not additional user approval steps.

### Person, headline, and spacing

- Choose a sharp, well-lit moment with open eyes, a natural expression, and a useful gesture when available. Extract from this video's cleaned A-roll without baked-in captions or cards; save the clean frame and timestamp.
- Keep the person's face, glasses, complete hair silhouette, clothing, and expression faithful to that frame. A cutout can be scaled down and repositioned; the person does not need to fill the cover or extend across its entire width. Do not invent a different performance or replace the user with another person.
- Start with a dedicated upper headline zone and a smaller half-body portrait in the middle/lower region, adjusting left/right placement around the topic imagery. A head width around one third of the cover is a useful starting point, not a fixed requirement. Keep the person recognizable at thumbnail size.
- Protect the entire head, including the top and outer edges of the hair, ears, glasses, and face. Leave a visible gap between the headline/marker block and the hair. Do not hide hair under text, a fading title background, a card, or a glow. If the headline collides, reduce/reposition the portrait or reflow the title; do not solve the collision by cropping the head or fading its upper edge away.
- Put the central question or payoff into a concise headline, normally one to three lines, with heavy high-contrast type and one decisive color emphasis. A small eyebrow and short footer are optional. Keep brand cards, headline, and footer in distinct reading zones rather than stacking them over the face.
- Match the title to the actual claim and inspect it at phone-grid thumbnail size. Borrow hierarchy and contrast from references, not another creator's identity, watermark, or exact composition.

### Topic-led background and brand elements

- For AI/software/technology topics, default to a restrained technology background: deep navy/blue-violet, a fine grid, a few data/circuit connections, subtle document/code motifs, and controlled depth or edge light. Keep the title region quiet and the portrait naturally lit. Use the project's palette for accents; warm Paper remains an option when it better fits the topic. Do not force a technology backdrop onto unrelated stories.
- This cover-specific treatment may use a few dimensional or softly lit brand cards. Keep those effects subordinate to the headline and real person; the video itself retains its editorial card/motion grammar. Replace generic background icons with meaningful topic elements rather than simply adding more stickers.
- Extract relevant company/product names from the current video's subject and the user's explicit visual brief. Proactively include recognizable official logos, wordmarks, product screenshots, or small product cards when they improve recognition. Scale and place them in free side/background areas, away from the whole head and headline. Give the main subject greater visual weight and other ecosystem brands secondary weight.
- In the saved Skills example, Anthropic is the main company and OpenAI, Cursor, and Grok are contextual AI-tool elements requested by the user. This is an example, not a fixed four-brand template for future topics. Choose brands relevant to each new story; avoid unrelated popular logos used only as decoration.
- Preserve accurate brand names, logo geometry, proportions, and colors. Prefer an official asset or clean wordmark over an invented or malformed icon; inspect any generated logos and spelling before delivery. When using image editing, provide the clean video frame as the identity reference and the existing cover as the layout target; follow the image skill, retain the final prompt, and save the result in the project.
- Separate topic context from factual relationships. Brand proximity or a decorative ecosystem backdrop must not be turned into a claim of donation, endorsement, partnership, ownership, or compatibility. Use directional arrows, handshakes, or other relationship symbols only for the relation established in the source. In the Skills example, `Anthropic → AAIF` is distinct from the surrounding tool-brand context; do not point all four brands into the donation or claim universal Skills support.
- Keep subject recognition, face likeness, and headline readability above background spectacle. Simplify or remove a brand module when it crowds the composition.

### Delivery

Use one finished 3:4 portrait cover by default, unless the user requests another size or alternatives. Save the selected source frame/timestamp, final image, and editable layout or generation prompt when applicable. Keep revisions as versioned siblings until the user requests replacement. Cover-only cutout, resizing, background replacement, and cropping do not authorize changes to the video master's framing.
