# Matching Cover

## Mandatory cover workflow for all three editing modes

Complete this workflow for every full classic (简洁剪视频), premium-v2 (精剪视频), and premium-v2.1-experimental (精剪视频2.1) package unless the user explicitly excludes the cover. The cover uses a faithful real-frame reference, a large topic-specific title, and a visual that conveys the story. The [technology-cover reference](../assets/cover-reference-tech-brands.png) and [creator-cover reference](../assets/cover-reference-creator-grid.png) illustrate hierarchy, title clearance, and recognizable subject elements. Choose a palette and setting for the current story.

### Plan → production → acceptance

1. **Plan:** visually inspect the relevant reference and selected clean A-roll frame. Record the story angle, main visual and why it fits, dominant background and accent, how the portrait light fits the scene, headline zone, exact headline, source frame/time, assets and provenance in DESIGN.md. Cover composition is shared across video variants. Follow any explicit cover instruction.
2. **Produce:** extract the chosen frame locally and use built-in ImageGen for a new cover or full redesign. Give the real frame as the identity reference and identify separate topic imagery or style references by role. Supply the exact headline, intended line breaks, and cover ratio. Use the plan's topic object, palette, and title zone. A rejected cover is a correction target only. Use authentic brand assets or accurate wordmarks and identify illustrations that could be mistaken for evidence.
3. **Accept:** inspect the actual final image beside the reference and source frame at full size and phone-grid thumbnail size. Record a result and brief visual evidence for every applicable Cover acceptance item in the project's validation report; explain any not-applicable item. Fix failures before delivery. Generation success or a more attractive revision cannot substitute for this check. These are agent checks, not additional user approval steps.

Built-in ImageGen is the approved cover generator, separate from ChatCut speech cleanup. Record ImageGen as the route and record a model name only when the tool exposes it. If the user explicitly requests a different route or exact model, verify that it is available and follow that request. Report a blocked cover stage when the required generator is unavailable. For an isolated frame swap, title correction, or layout adjustment, reuse the editable cover when it can make the requested change.

### Person, headline, and spacing

- Compare several sharp, well-lit frames around pauses and sentence endings. Prefer open eyes, relaxed brows and jaw, and a naturally resting mouth or subtle smile. Skip clenched teeth, pronounced grimaces, and exaggerated mid-syllable expressions. Prioritize a calm expression over a larger gesture. Save the chosen frame and timestamp from this video's cleaned A-roll.
- Keep the person's face, glasses, complete hair silhouette, clothing, and expression faithful to that frame. A cutout can be scaled down and repositioned; the person does not need to fill the cover or extend across its entire width. Do not invent a different performance or replace the user with another person.
- Reserve a quiet title zone above the scene when the headline carries the story, or below when the subject visual should lead. A title integrated into a physical prop can work if every word survives thumbnail reduction. Place the recognizable half-body portrait beside the main subject and retain a useful gesture where available.
- Protect the entire head, including the top and outer edges of the hair, ears, glasses, and face. Leave a visible gap between the headline/marker block and the hair. Do not hide hair under text, a fading title background, a card, or a glow. If the headline collides, reduce/reposition the portrait or reflow the title; do not solve the collision by cropping the head or fading its upper edge away.
- Put the central question or payoff into a concise headline, normally one to three lines, with heavy high-contrast type and one decisive color emphasis. A small eyebrow and short footer are optional. Keep brand cards, headline, and footer in distinct reading zones rather than stacking them over the face.
- Match the title to the actual claim and inspect it at phone-grid thumbnail size. Borrow hierarchy and contrast from references, not another creator's identity, watermark, or exact composition.

### Choose the scene and palette for the story

- Let the video's specific premise drive the setting. A guide or workflow can use a workbench, field manual, or stack of steps. A tool selection can show a small collection of app icons with a shared organizing device. A news or market story can feature one evidence object and a chart or directional cue. A creative process can put the resulting world or object on display. A language or audience shift can use a sign, ticket, or other recognizable cue to the transition.
- Choose one dominant background family, a readable title color, and one primary accent that works with the portrait's light. Examples include pine green with amber for a guide, burgundy with mint for a curated tool list, charcoal with yellow for an urgent event, electric blue for a live systems experiment, warm amber for handcrafted scenes, and deep teal with orange for travel or reach. These are options. Broad labels like “AI” do not select the color or require a grid.
- Give the scene depth with a quiet background, one clear topic object, and the actual presenter. Add props only where they help the viewer identify the subject. Real screenshots, editorial illustrations and brand marks can coexist when they have distinct roles. Keep decorative marks below the title and face in the visual hierarchy.
- In the approved workflow-cover example, a task sheet, a replay strip, and a check stamp form one story about verifying work. A calm portrait sits beside the evidence, with two large title lines in a clear zone. Use this balance of subject, person, and title as the standard. Choose new props and colors for each video.
- Extract relevant company/product names from the current video's subject and the user's explicit visual brief. Proactively include recognizable official logos, wordmarks, product screenshots, or small product cards when they improve recognition. Scale and place them in free side/background areas, away from the whole head and headline. Give the main subject greater visual weight and other ecosystem brands secondary weight.
- In the saved Skills example, Anthropic is the main company and OpenAI, Cursor, and Grok are contextual AI-tool elements requested by the user. This is an example, not a fixed four-brand template for future topics. Choose brands relevant to each new story; avoid unrelated popular logos used only as decoration.
- Preserve accurate brand names, logo geometry, proportions, and colors. Prefer an official asset or clean wordmark over an invented or malformed icon; inspect any generated logos and spelling before delivery. When using image editing, provide the clean video frame as the identity reference and the existing cover as the layout target; follow the image skill, retain the final prompt, and save the result in the project.
- Separate topic context from factual relationships. Brand proximity or a decorative ecosystem backdrop must not be turned into a claim of donation, endorsement, partnership, ownership, or compatibility. Use directional arrows, handshakes, or other relationship symbols only for the relation established in the source. In the Skills example, `Anthropic → AAIF` is distinct from the surrounding tool-brand context; do not point all four brands into the donation or claim universal Skills support.
- In the phone-grid check, read the headline first, identify the topic object, and recognize the person. If two visual elements compete, simplify the scene or remove a secondary element. Keep essential content away from likely interface overlays near the corners and bottom.

### Delivery

For full packaging in all three modes, deliver both a finished 3:4 portrait cover and a finished 4:3 landscape cover by default (width:height). Follow explicit requests for one version, different ratios, or no cover. For a cover-only request, deliver the requested versions.

Recompose each version for its canvas. Use the same exact title, calm source frame, topic visual, and palette across the two versions. When making a landscape companion to an approved portrait cover, provide the portrait cover as the design reference and the real frame as the separate identity reference for ImageGen. Reflow the title and reposition the subject and topic elements to fit the wider canvas; avoid stretching or directly cropping the finished portrait cover to fit. Check actual pixel dimensions for the exact ratio and review each final at full size and phone-grid size.

Save the selected source frame/timestamp, each final image with its ratio or orientation in its filename, and the editable layout or generation prompt for each version. Keep revisions as versioned siblings until the user requests replacement. Cover-only cutout, resizing, background replacement, and cropping do not authorize changes to the video master's framing.
